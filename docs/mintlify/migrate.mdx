# Migrate to Robin

Already launched a token somewhere else? You can relaunch it native on Robinhood Chain in minutes, keep your name, ticker, and logo, and carry a verifiable record of where you came from. This page is the whole migration kit: why move, the exact steps, the checklists, and how to reach a human.

There is one rail. A migrated coin launches through the same fair launch curve every other Robin coin uses. No bespoke contract, no special path, and you keep your owner wallet the whole way.

## Why migrate

- **Native to Robinhood Chain.** You launch directly on Robinhood Chain, an Arbitrum Orbit L2 built for speed, not bolted on as an afterthought.
- **Low, clear fees.** A flat 1% per side, shown up front, with no hidden cuts buried in the flow.
- **Holders earn.** Trading activity routes real ETH back to net-volume traders and diamond-hand holders, so your community is paid to stay. See [Holder & Trader Rewards](rewards.md).
- **Migrated From badge.** Your coin carries a verifiable provenance badge showing its origin, so buyers trust this is the real relaunch and not a copycat.
- **One paste import.** Drop in your old contract and we pull your name, ticker, and logo from DexScreener, so you never rebuild your identity from scratch.
- **A real floor at graduation.** Fill the curve and your coin graduates with 0.5 ETH to the creator plus a permanent, locked liquidity floor. See [Graduation](graduation.md).

## Step by step

You do not need to write any code.

1. **Open the migrate flow.** Go to [robinlab.io/migrate](https://robinlab.io/migrate.html) and select Start migration, or open the create page and expand the "Coming from another chain or pad?" panel.
2. **Paste your old token.** Enter the contract address of the token you launched elsewhere, on whatever chain or pad it lives on now.
3. **Pull your metadata.** Click import. We read your project's public data from DexScreener and fill in your name, ticker, and logo for you.
4. **Review everything.** Check that the name, ticker, and logo are correct. Fix anything that looks off, and add your description and social links.
5. **Connect your wallet.** Connect the wallet you want to control the new coin. This wallet becomes the owner of the relaunched token on Robinhood Chain.
6. **Launch.** Confirm in your wallet. The fee shown on screen is the total, with nothing added after. Your coin goes live on the curve.
7. **Save your profile with the badge.** Once live, save your coin profile. The Migrated From badge attaches automatically and records where your token originated. Only the coin's own creator can set it, so it cannot be faked.
8. **Run the go-live checklist below** before you announce.

## FAQ

**Does migrating move my old liquidity?**
No. Migrating relaunches your token native on Robinhood Chain as a fresh launch on the curve. Your old liquidity stays where it is on the old chain, and you decide how and when to move your community over.

**What happens to my old token?**
It keeps existing on its original chain. Nothing we do touches or removes it. The new token is the one that lives on Robinhood Chain and carries your community forward, with the Migrated From badge pointing back to the original as proof of where you started.

**Is the Migrated From badge automatic?**
Yes. When you import your old contract and launch through the migrate flow, the badge attaches to your coin profile automatically and records your origin. The badge is part of the creator-signed profile, so only the coin's own dev can set it.

**Can my old-chain holders buy in?**
Yes. Every migrated coin page shows a "Buy from any chain" shortcut that routes through the bridge, so holders can come across with the token they already hold. The destination is always Robinhood Chain.

**What does it cost?**
You pay a launch fee plus the standard 1% per side trading fee, both shown clearly on screen before you confirm. There are no hidden cuts added after the fact.

**How long does it take?**
The core migration takes minutes. Paste, import, review, connect, launch. Most of your time goes into the parts you control, like reviewing details and planning your announcement, not the technical steps.
